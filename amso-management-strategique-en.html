1. Foundations of Strategic Management

DefinitionStrategic management is the coordinated process of formulating, implementing and evaluating major cross-functional decisions so that an organization can achieve its long-term objectives and maintain a competitive position.
PurposeIts purpose is to help the organization make coherent choices under uncertainty: where to compete, how to compete, what to prioritize and what not to do.
BenefitsThe course distinguishes financial benefits (sales, profitability, productivity and long-term performance) from non-financial benefits such as discipline, coordination, communication, forward thinking, synergy and better allocation of resources.
Key termsCompetitive advantage, strategists, mission, business, external opportunities and threats, internal strengths and weaknesses, long-term objectives, annual objectives, strategies and policies.

2. Strategic Diagnosis & Environmental Analysis

Main thinkersThe course mobilizes major contributions from Ansoff, Drucker, Schumpeter, Mintzberg and Porter to explain planning, innovation, deliberate/emergent strategy, competitive forces and competitive advantage.
Business, mission and objectivesThe business describes what the firm does and for whom; the mission expresses why it exists; objectives translate strategic intent into results to be achieved.
Strategic decisionsStrategic decisions concern the long term, are difficult to reverse and are made under uncertainty. Mintzberg distinguishes deliberate strategy from emergent strategy.
DiagnosisInternal diagnosis identifies strengths and weaknesses; external diagnosis identifies opportunities and threats. PESTEL helps scan the macro-environment, while SWOT interprets the strategic meaning of the findings.

3. Strategic Segmentation

PurposeStrategic segmentation identifies the activities for which a specific strategy must be developed. It supports competitive analysis, identification of Key Success Factors and subdivision of the firm into Strategic Business Units (SBUs).
CriteriaInternal criteria include technologies, competencies, synergies and cost structures. External criteria include customers, markets, distribution channels and competitors.
MethodsThe course presents segmentation by breakdown and grouping, Abell's three-dimensional approach (customers, needs, technologies/solutions), and the MIDO(F) method.

4. Types of Strategies

Porter's generic strategiesCompetitive advantage may be pursued through cost leadership, differentiation and focus. The strategic choice must fit the target market and the organization's capabilities.
Strategic choiceA strategy should connect diagnosis, competitive position, resources and the organization's long-term objectives.

5. Business Portfolio Analysis Models

Portfolio logicA business portfolio groups the activities or industries in which a company operates. Portfolio matrices compare SBUs using consistent criteria and help diagnose the strategic potential of the whole organization.
SPACE matrixThe SPACE matrix assesses strategic posture through internal dimensions (financial and competitive position) and external dimensions (industry and stability position), leading to aggressive, conservative, defensive or competitive orientations.
Other modelsThe module also refers to portfolio tools such as BCG, McKinsey and ADL for comparing activities and guiding investment, maintenance, development or withdrawal choices.

6. Strategic Planning

Planning processStrategic planning links internal diagnosis, external diagnosis, strategic choice, objective setting, strategy formulation, implementation and monitoring.
Continuous adaptationStrategy is not a one-off document. Organizations must monitor internal and external changes, evaluate results and take corrective action when necessary.
